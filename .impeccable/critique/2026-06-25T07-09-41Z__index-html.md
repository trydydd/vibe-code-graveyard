## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Does NOT look AI-generated. The obituary aesthetic with monospace-first typography, flat dark surfaces, and a single clinical red accent is a deliberate creative choice, not a training-data reflex. The contrast between deadpan design and absurd content is the brand itself. No gradient text, no glassmorphism, no card-grid template, no eyebrow kickers, no side-stripes. The site reads like it was designed by someone who actually understands the concept, not someone who generated "dark mode with red accents."

**Deterministic scan:** Detector returned zero anti-patterns (`[]`, exit code 0). Clean sweep.

**Verdict: CLEAN.** No AI slop detected. This is a distinctive brand surface.

---

## Overall Impression

The graveyard is sharp. The obituary metaphor is executed with genuine discipline — monospace as authority, flat surfaces, one accent color used sparingly. It doesn't look like anything else on the internet right now, which is exactly the point. The single biggest opportunity is utility: the site is a great static memorial but lacks the interactive features that would make it a functional tool for its audience (search, sort, filter persistence, better empty states).

---

## What's Working

1. **The monospace majority rule is nailed.** Headers, metadata, buttons, labels, counters — all mono. Sans-serif appears only for body paragraphs. This creates a terminal-report atmosphere that no other brand surface achieves.

2. **The Morgue Red constraint is respected.** The accent appears on links, active filters, the counter pill, and tool labels — nowhere else. Its rarity gives it weight. A screen full of red would kill the clinical tone.

3. **The entry card hierarchy is clean.** Name (white, bold, mono) → tool (red, uppercase) → description (gray, sans) → meta (dim mono). Four levels, three type scales, no competition. Each card reads like a case file.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Entry descriptions truncate mid-sentence with no ellipsis**
- **Why it matters:** The grid shows 12 entries, and at least 8 have descriptions cut off at the CSS `overflow: hidden` boundary with no `...` indicator. The user has no signal that there's more content, so they assume the entry is short. This kills the primary content of the site.
- **Fix:** Add `text-overflow: ellipsis` with a max line clamp, or better yet: truncate in JS at the JSON level to a fixed character budget that ends on a word boundary.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish index.html`

**[P0] Removal form uses `mailto:` — breaks on most email clients**
- **Why it matters:** The entire lead-gen mechanism is the removal form. `mailto:` opens a desktop email client, which most web users don't have configured. The form silently fails for anyone on mobile or Chrome. You lose the lead.
- **Fix:** Replace with a real form handler. Options: Formspree (free tier), Getform, or a simple Vercel Serverless Function. Even a Google Form embed beats `mailto:`.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable craft removal form`

**[P1] No filter persistence — refreshing the page resets to "All"**
- **Why it matters:** A founder finds a Cursor-specific entry, shares the link with their co-founder, and the co-founder lands on "All" instead. The URL doesn't reflect the filter state, so deep-linking is impossible.
- **Fix:** Update URL hash or search params on filter click: `?tool=cursor`. Read on page load.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish index.html`

**[P1] `#666` body text on `#0a0a0a` background fails WCAG AA**
- **Why it matters:** `#666` on `#0a0a0a` is approximately 2.8:1 contrast ratio. WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text. The tagline, about section copy, and footer text all use `--text-dim: #666`. On a dark background, this reads as washed out — exactly the pattern the design system warns against ("gray text on a colored background looks washed out").
- **Fix:** Bump `--text-dim` to at least `#888` (≈4.2:1) or `#999` (≈5.3:1). Or use a transparency of the primary text color: `color: rgba(200, 200, 200, 0.6)` instead of a hard gray.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit a11y`

**[P2] Entry counter doesn't update when filtered**
- **Why it matters:** The hero says "12 startups in the ground" even when the user has filtered to "Claude Code" and sees 0 results. The mismatch between the big counter and the filtered view creates confusion.
- **Fix:** Show filtered count: "3 of 12 shown" or update to show the filtered subset.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish index.html`

## Persona Red Flags

**Solo Founder (primary — vibe-coded their app, embarrassed, looking for help)**
- No red flags on the homepage. They see their situation documented clinically, not mocked. The "Get removed →" link in the footer is the right CTA — it implies help without stating it.
- One concern: they might not realize the graveyard owner offers services. The footer says "Contact: email" but never explicitly connects the graveyard to cleanup services. The implicit connection is clever but might be too subtle for someone in panic mode.

**Non-Technical Co-Founder (secondary — inherited the app, needs a translator)**
- No red flags. The clinical tone makes it approachable — this isn't a developer-only space.

**Agency Owner (tertiary — shipped an AI-built client app that's breaking)**
- Red flag: no way to discover that the graveyard owner actually offers cleanup services beyond the removal form. An agency owner browsing might think this is just a community project and never reach out.

## Minor Observations

- The skull emoji (☠) as header icon is on-brand but could be more distinctive. Consider a custom SVG skull that matches the monospace aesthetic (pixel-art or wireframe style).
- Entry cards that have no description text (some entries show an empty `<p>`) still render the paragraph element with zero height — creates a tiny gap. Filter those out or show "No public description available."
- The `fadeIn` animation on entry cards runs on every page load, which is fine, but with 12 entries they all animate simultaneously. A staggered delay (`animation-delay: calc(var(--index) * 0.05s)`) would feel more polished.
- Mobile: the filter buttons wrap to multiple rows. At 480px, they're cramped but functional. Consider horizontal scroll with snap points on mobile instead of wrapping.

## Questions to Consider

- "What if the graveyard had a search bar? Founders Googling their own app name would land here and immediately find themselves — the shame hook becomes instant."
- "Should each entry have a 'technical debt score'? A visual metric (like a credit score for codebases) would make the graveyard feel more forensic and less like a list."
- "What if the 'Get removed' flow had an intermediate step? Instead of going straight to the form, a 'Find your entry →' page that asks for the app name and highlights the matching grave. One more moment of recognition before the escape hatch."
